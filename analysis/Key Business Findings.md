Key Business Findings
1. Executive Summary

The Business Performance Analytics project analyzes sales, revenue, profit, customers, products, regions, sales channels, and discounting behavior across 2022–2025.

Overall business performance remained relatively stable during the period, with annual revenue staying close to $25 million. However, the analysis identified a significant profitability risk associated with higher discount levels.

The strongest business opportunity is to improve discount management while protecting revenue and maintaining profitable sales.

2. Revenue and Profit Performance

Annual performance remained relatively stable between 2022 and 2025.

Year	Revenue	Profit	Profit Margin
2022	$25.18M	$8.96M	35.57%
2023	$25.45M	$9.07M	35.64%
2024	$25.07M	$8.86M	35.32%
2025	$24.69M	$8.85M	35.85%

2023 produced the highest annual revenue and profit.

Revenue subsequently declined, reaching approximately $24.69 million in 2025. Despite the decline in revenue, the overall profit margin remained close to 36%.

Business implication

The business does not show a dramatic deterioration in profitability, but the decline in revenue after 2023 suggests that management should investigate the factors affecting sales growth.

3. Category Performance

Home generated the highest revenue among the four categories.

Category	Revenue	Profit	Profit Margin
Home	$27.23M	$9.64M	35.39%
Furniture	$25.27M	$8.94M	35.37%
Technology	$24.08M	$8.14M	33.82%
Office Supplies	$23.81M	$9.01M	37.86%

Home is the strongest category by revenue.

Office Supplies has the highest profit margin, despite generating less revenue than Home and Furniture.

Technology has the lowest profit margin at approximately 33.8%.

Business implication

Revenue leadership and profitability leadership are not the same.

Management should therefore evaluate categories using both revenue and margin rather than revenue alone.

Technology may require additional analysis of pricing, product costs, and discounting to understand its lower profitability.

4. Regional Performance

The West and East regions generated the highest revenue.

Region	Revenue	Profit	Profit Margin
West	$23.51M	$8.40M	35.71%
East	$23.50M	$8.36M	35.57%
North	$20.58M	$7.33M	35.63%
South	$18.56M	$6.60M	35.56%
Central	$14.25M	$5.05M	35.43%

The West region has the highest revenue and profit, while Central has the lowest.

Profit margins are relatively consistent across regions, ranging from approximately 35.4% to 35.7%.

Business implication

Regional differences are primarily driven by sales volume rather than major differences in profitability.

The Central and South regions may provide opportunities for growth through customer acquisition, sales expansion, or channel development.

5. Sales Channel Performance

Online is the dominant sales channel.

Channel	Revenue	Profit	Orders	Customers
Online	$64.79M	$23.09M	16,771	4,850
Retail	$35.60M	$12.64M	9,195	4,215

Online generated substantially more revenue, profit, and orders than Retail.

Profit margins are very similar between the two channels:

Online: 35.64%
Retail: 35.51%
Business implication

Online is the primary revenue engine and should remain an important area for investment.

Because profitability is similar across both channels, the difference in performance is primarily related to sales volume rather than significantly better margins.

6. Discounting and Profitability

Discounting is the most significant profitability risk identified in the analysis.

Discount Band	Revenue	Profit Margin	Loss Rate
0%	$28.00M	41.42%	0.00%
1–10%	$45.45M	36.61%	0.00%
11–20%	$23.09M	29.67%	3.24%
21–30%	$3.85M	16.75%	22.24%

Profitability decreases as discount levels increase.

The highest discount band has a profit margin of only 16.75%, compared with 41.42% for transactions with no discount.

More importantly, the loss rate rises dramatically:

0% discount: 0% loss rate
1–10% discount: 0% loss rate
11–20% discount: 3.24% loss rate
21–30% discount: 22.24% loss rate
Business implication

Discounts above 20% should be treated as a significant profitability risk.

The business should consider approval thresholds, minimum-margin rules, or targeted discounting rather than applying large discounts broadly.

7. Negative-Profit Transactions

There were:

1,147 negative-profit transactions
1.91% of all transactions

Although the overall percentage is relatively small, the discount analysis shows that losses are concentrated in higher discount bands.

This suggests that negative-profit transactions should not be viewed only as isolated transactions. They should also be analyzed in relation to discounting, products, customers, regions, and sales channels.

Business implication

A useful management control would be to monitor transactions where discounts are high and profit becomes negative.

8. Key Business Recommendations
Recommendation 1 — Control high discounts

Introduce stronger controls for discounts above 20%.

Possible approaches include:

Manager approval for high discounts
Minimum-margin requirements
Discount limits by product category
Monitoring loss-making transactions
Recommendation 2 — Investigate Technology profitability

Technology generates significant revenue but has the lowest category profit margin.

Further analysis should examine:

Product-level margins
Average discount
Unit costs
Pricing
Loss-making products
Recommendation 3 — Protect the Online channel

Online is the dominant sales channel and generates approximately $64.8 million in revenue.

The business should continue investing in the channel while monitoring customer acquisition cost, retention, and profitability.

Recommendation 4 — Investigate lower-performing regions

Central and South have lower revenue than West and East.

Management should investigate whether the difference is driven by:

Customer base size
Order frequency
Product mix
Sales channel adoption
Regional pricing or discounting
Recommendation 5 — Focus on profitable growth

Revenue growth should not be pursued through excessive discounting.

The analysis indicates that increasing discounts can substantially reduce margins and increase loss-making transactions.

9. Limitations

This analysis has several limitations.

The dataset represents a defined historical period from 2022–2025.
The analysis identifies relationships but does not establish causation.
Discounting and profitability are strongly associated in the dataset, but other factors may also influence losses.
Customer behavior and product-level drivers require additional investigation.
The dataset does not necessarily represent a real company's complete operational environment.

Therefore, the findings should be treated as analytical insights rather than definitive causal conclusions.

10. Overall Conclusion

The business demonstrates relatively stable revenue and profitability across the four-year period.

The strongest revenue contributors are Home, Online sales, and the West and East regions.

However, the most important profitability finding is the relationship between discounting and losses. Discounts above 20% are associated with substantially lower profit margins and a 22.24% loss rate.

The primary business opportunity is therefore to achieve profitable growth by improving discount controls while continuing to strengthen high-performing categories, channels, and regions.
